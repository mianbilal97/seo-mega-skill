# Competitor evidence, identity and recovery

Apply these branches to audits and strategies when the user supplies the relevant data or asks for research. Use the advanced notes as practitioner examples. Anchor implementation decisions to direct evidence and current policy.

## Compare what would actually be required to compete

1. Compare sources in the same market and task. Record shared and unique queries, representative URLs, inventory/interaction, freshness where relevant, proof and what source answered the task better. Distinguish traffic estimates, rank observations and Search Console data.
2. For query exports record normalization, source dates, coverage limits, language and location. Show shared queries relative to a declared denominator such as the site's comparable export or the union. Compare both same task and same page type. Query overlap and UR/DR can help choose benchmarks. Estimated traffic value is neither the cost to outrank a site nor proof of relevance.
3. For backlink exports deduplicate referring domains and live links at the appropriate level. Review topical relevance, placement, whether the link is live, editorial context and commercial relationship. Keep discovered domains and worthwhile outreach targets distinct. Show low/no-data cases as such. Score fit from what readers need and what the site can genuinely contribute. Reject profile drops, paid links for ranking, anonymous promotion and bulk link swaps.
4. Find the missing information, not just missing terms. For each meaningful task write a side-by-side evidence table with source, competing answer or capability, verified weakness, proposed improvement and the cost or prerequisites. Gaps may involve a better data source, an exact part cross-reference, a valid test, a clearer definition under a condition or an actual purchase or productivity interaction.
5. Use a small meaningful comparison table or chart when asked. For competitor overlap show labeled query counts and percentages. For links show profile counts by disclosed bins with separate evidence-quality observations. For cluster summaries show query count and demand estimate separately. Keep labels, ranges and denominators readable.

## Establish a trustworthy brand and authorship

- Audit actual branded queries and prompts by locale. Include navigational intent and questions about pricing, reliability or support. Record the official page, main independent sources, common mismatches, visible result features and current action. Identify name collisions and use product, place or category context to distinguish identities.
- Put verifiable names, company role and relevant support, editorial or product disclosures where visitors need them. Ensure About, contact, service and author pages support the actual task and match known evidence. Include policy pages only where the site and jurisdiction call for them. Use trustworthy external corroboration and accurate identifying references.
- For an existing Knowledge Panel, inspect what is displayed and use the current Google claim/feedback route when applicable and authorized. Do not create panel claims or false identity links. Keep panel appearance as an observed feature rather than a KPI guarantee.
- Use bylines, author/reviewer pages, original evidence, testing details and editorial methods where they help users assess the claim. Treat E-E-A-T language as a quality assessment framework. Never invent author experience, reviewer credentials, certifications or a numerical E-E-A-T score.
- For local operating businesses verify eligibility, representation, real location or service coverage, customer contact, hours and accurate business details across the website and legitimate listings. Assess Maps and organic performance separately. Baseline branded and service queries at appropriate locations. Prioritize relevant partnerships, useful local assets and legitimate customer feedback.

## Diagnose traffic declines and template risk with cohorts

1. Align change dates, affected templates and market. Segment branded/unbranded and query/page coverage where possible. Check manual actions or security notices if access exists. Check tracking changes, release history, deployment and indexing/canonical issues first.
2. Compare representative affected and stable cohorts. Distinguish fewer impressions, weaker positions on comparable queries, reduced CTR at a similar position, lost conversions and changes in which queries enter averages. Consider seasonality and changing SERP layouts. Treat an algorithm update's timing as a potential confounder, not a diagnosis.
3. Compare mobile screens and click paths around the largest observed friction. Separate site issues from search demand and what competitors improved. Review crawl logs or Crawl Stats when available, using the site's verified bot methodology. Assess response types, latency, discovery and resource constraints against the site's intended architecture and historical baseline. Avoid copying arbitrary crawl percentages or response time targets from case notes.
4. For low-value or thin pages identify missing purpose, facts, functioning UI or assets, and check incoming links, conversions and intent before removing or redirecting. Useful short content, expected exclusions and recently introduced pages can be correct. Try substantive improvements or eligible consolidation and measure the treated group. Avoid migrations done solely to escape visibility losses.
5. For programmatic pages set a required verified field set and source ownership, with deterministic empty, expired, duplicate and out-of-scope handling. Sample sparse records, canonical combinations and unsupported markets. Pilot a small cohort and check discoverability, task completion and ongoing update capacity before expanding.
6. Track initial discovery and movement as observations. Mark deployment, first observed indexing and first observed impression when available. Compare stable periods, preserve positive controls and exclude unavailable observations from conclusions. Avoid fixed re-ranking timelines or claims about hidden quality scores.
